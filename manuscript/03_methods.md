## Study design

<!-- 3b, 4, 5a, 14c, 14d -->

This was an offline evaluation of a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipeline, using retrospectively collected, de-identified routine newborn-unit records. It had two parts:
1. **Primary:** a cross-sectional, multi-rater clinician evaluation of the pipeline's answers for 200 records.
2. **Secondary:** an exploratory analysis of retrieval query construction for 933 other records.

No answer reached clinicians caring for the patients. We follow TRIPOD-LLM [REF: TRIPOD-LLM, Nat Med, doi:10.1038/s41591-024-03425-5 — authors/year to verify]. The evaluation adapts the PATH LLM Playbook [REF: PATH LLM Playbook] and Mwaniki et al. [REF: doi:10.1136/bmjdh-2026-000081]. The study was not registered, and no study protocol was published.

## Data sources

<!-- 5a, 5b, 5c, 5d -->

Records came from the Clinical Information Network (CIN), which collects standardised routine admission and discharge data from newborn units in 21 public county hospitals in 14 of Kenya's 47 counties [REF: Tuti T, Muema T, English M, Clinical Information Network Group, Aluvaala J. A cluster analysis of neonates using clinical signs of possible serious bacterial infection at hospital admission in Kenya: a retrospective multicentre cohort study. PLoS One. 2026;21(9):e0359212. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0359212 — verified via Europe PMC PMC13606953] [CITE: original CIN methods paper]. The extract came from 8 of these hospitals, the same hospitals that formed the development cohort of the triage-cluster study described below. As in that study, the source was limited to neonates with at least one sign of possible serious bacterial infection (pSBI) at admission, so every record represents a suspected neonatal infection. These hospitals had 44,626 such newborn-unit admissions between January 2021 and December 2024. We excluded 237 admissions with more than 30% missing data across 14 pSBI-related clinical features. From the remaining 44,389, one admission was kept per unique combination of those features, leaving 7,994. From these, 300 admissions were sampled at random (seed 20261013) in each of five triage categories, Critical, Substantial, Moderate, Low and Minimal, giving 1,500 records (18.76% of 7,994) with direct identifiers removed (Additional file 1, S1.1). The triage categories are mortality-risk clusters from a latent class model of pSBI signs recorded at admission, developed and externally validated in CIN data [REF: Tuti et al. 2026, as above]. Categories were assigned to these records by applying that model. Each record was one admission snapshot with 39 variables:
- demographics;
- presenting complaint;
- admission vital signs;
- examination findings and maternal risk factors, each recorded as present, absent or not assessed.

The triage category was used only for sampling and for the triage-agreement question; it never entered a question or query.

The guideline collection comprised four documents on neonatal infection [CITE: each]:
- WHO recommendations on serious bacterial infection in infants aged 0–59 days (2024);
- Kenya's Comprehensive Newborn Care Protocols (2022);
- Kenya's National Antibiotic Use Guidelines (2024);
- NICE NG195 (2021, updated 2026).

Except for NG195, only excerpts were used. Documents were split by structure (recommendations, protocol steps, tables, prose windows) into 297 chunks. Of these, 153 were retrievable once navigation, front-matter and rationale sections were excluded (Additional file 1, S1.2).

## Pipeline under evaluation

<!-- 6a, 6b, 6c, 6d, 9a, 9b -->

The pipeline answers only from the indexed guidelines. It reports and cites what they say and gives no independent clinical advice (Additional file 1, S1.3–S1.4).

**Retrieval:**
- Each guideline was searched separately, with dense (bge-large-en-v1.5) and sparse lexical search merged by reciprocal rank fusion.
- Candidates were re-ranked with a cross-encoder (bge-reranker-v2-m3).
- From each guideline, up to 3–5 chunks scoring at least 0.30 were kept, depending on the query area.
- Local guidelines were presented before international ones.
- If retrieval confidence was low, the pipeline returned "no guideline found".

**Generation and grounding:**
- Answers were generated by qwen3.6:35b [REF: Qwen Team. Qwen3.6-35B-A3B: agentic coding power, now open to all [Internet]. April 2026. Available from: https://qwen.ai/blog?id=qwen3.6-35b-a3b — supplied by PI 2026-10-27] (Q4_K_M quantisation), served locally by Ollama 0.32.14 through the BRIDGE LLM Gateway (release 1.0) on an NVIDIA H200 GPU [REF: gateway repository]. The model, released on 16 April 2026, was used without fine-tuning. The developers have not published a training-data cut-off for this model.
- Settings: 32,768-token context window, thinking mode off. The platform sent no sampling settings, so the gateway defaults applied: temperature 0.0, top-p 1.0, top-k 1, seed 42 and a cap of 4,096 generated tokens (Ollama num_predict). With top-k 1 the model always selects its most likely next token (greedy decoding).
- The prompts were fixed templates written during platform development and were not tuned on the study records (Additional file 2).
- The model answered from the retrieved passages only, giving each statement a citation and a verbatim quote.
- A deterministic gate released a statement only if its citation pointed to a passage retrieved for that question, its quote appeared verbatim in the passage, and the quote supported the statement. Support was judged by word overlap between the statement and its quote, with a constrained model call when the overlap was inconclusive. Directive wording was also blocked.
- Unsupported statements were removed. If an answer could not be released safely, the question was escalated.
- Every released answer carried a fixed disclaimer.

Answers were generated on 14–16 October 2026, and no fallback model was used.

## Questions generated from routine records

<!-- 5d, 9b, 10 -->

Each record produced one templated question per query area. The four areas are aspects of suspected neonatal infection:
- assessment;
- investigations;
- severity classification;
- antibiotic course.

A question opened with the area's fixed wording, written by the principal investigator ("What does the guideline recommend about … based only on the content provided below:"). The patient details the area lists then followed:
- **Assessment, investigations and severity classification:** day of life, gestational age, presenting complaint, maternal risk factors, vital signs and examination findings.
- **Antibiotic course:** age, gestation, weight and care setting.

Missing or not-assessed fields were omitted. Nothing was imputed (Additional file 1, S1.5).

## Study samples

<!-- 5a, 5e, 16a -->

**Clinician evaluation.** We drew 200 records (40 per triage category) at random, using a fixed seed. Each record produced four items, giving 800 items. Every item was kept whatever the pipeline's outcome.

**Ablation.** The remaining 1,300 records were eligible. Each record, taken in random order, contributed one question, assigned to the area with the fewest usable questions so far.

In both samples, records that nearly duplicated one already selected were skipped. The two samples share no records.

Of the 1,300 eligible records, 933 patient records were used for the ablation study after 320/1300 records were skipped after being judged as near-duplicates, and a further 47/1300 questions (all on antibiotic query area) whose answer cited nothing  i.e. had no gold set, were dropped (Figure 1; Additional file 1, S1.6).

## Clinician evaluation

<!-- 7a, 7d, 7e, 8a, 8b, 8c, 11 -->

**What raters saw.** Raters worked independently in a web application. For each item they saw:
- the question;
- the answer, with its cited quotes and their sources;
- the disclaimer.

[TODO: confirm what the review page shows.] There was no comparator.

**Allocation.** Items were allocated so that each would receive three independent ratings, with no consensus step (Additional file 1, S1.7).

**Rubric.** Each answer was scored on the 11 domains of the PATH LLM rubric, using its definitions and 5-point anchors (Additional file 1, Table S1):
1. alignment with medical consensus;
2. question comprehension;
3. knowledge recall;
4. logical reasoning;
5. inclusion of irrelevant content;
6. omission of important information;
7. possible extent of harm;
8. possible likelihood of harm;
9. clear communication;
10. understanding of local context;
11. potential for demographic bias.

For every domain, 1 is the worst score and 5 the best. "No guideline found" and escalation outcomes were also scored. The 11 domains carry equal weight.

**Accept decision.** Raters also recorded whether they would accept the answer in full or in part, reject it, or judge the question out of scope.

**Triage agreement.** This secondary assessment asked whether clinicians accept the mortality-risk category assigned by the latent class model [REF: Tuti et al. 2026]. It was limited to the 200 assessment items (40 per triage category), because the assessment question states the record's admission findings; the triage question was not asked on the other three areas. The question stated only the findings that had been assessed, as present or absent; findings that were not assessed or were missing were omitted, so raters could see fewer of the 14 features than the model used. After rating the answer and recording the accept decision, raters read a fixed text: "This newborn was assigned triage category [category] based on the information available at the time of admission. In the PLOS ONE study (doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0359212), newborns in this category had a mortality rate of [mortality]. How strongly do you agree with this triage category, given the clinical features shown?" The mortality was that category's in-hospital mortality in the study's development cohort, shown rounded to the nearest percent (Critical 67.80%, Substantial 42.47%, Moderate 9.56%, Low 2.86%, Minimal 0.98%, shown as ~68%, ~42%, ~10%, ~3% and ~1%), with a note that it was a local statistic, not guideline content. Raters answered on a 5-point Likert scale (1 strongly disagree, 2 disagree, 3 neither agree nor disagree, 4 agree, 5 strongly agree); the end labels were always visible and all five could be expanded on screen. A response was required on every assessment item. The three raters of each item answered independently, giving up to 600 ratings. The category never appeared in a question, a retrieval query or an answer (Additional file 1, Table S1).

## Retrieval ablation

<!-- 6e, 7a, 7b, 7c, 7e -->

The ablation asked how the retrieval of guideline passages depends on the way a query is built from the record. To vary each component on its own, it used an offline retriever that ranks all 153 chunks.

**Gold standards:**
- **Primary:** each question's gold set was the passages cited by the platform's own answer. This measures recovery of the evidence the platform used, not clinical correctness.
- **Secondary:** reference passage sets for each area, curated by the principal investigator.

**Factors.** Three factors were crossed:
1. **Finding handling:** present findings only, or all assessed findings. These are identical for antibiotic course, which states no findings.
2. **Enrichment:** the query as written, or with terms added from a 22-concept clinical vocabulary for fields the question states.
3. **Lexical–semantic weight w:** each chunk was scored as w × Okapi BM25 + (1 − w) × SapBERT similarity [CITE: BM25; SapBERT], for w = 0 to 1 in steps of 0.2.

The area's opening wording was weighted three-fold in all conditions.

**Metrics and comparator.**
- The headline metric was MRR@10, the reciprocal rank of the first gold passage within the top 10.
- Recall@10 and K = 2–20 are reported alongside.
- The comparator was a query-blind "popularity" ranking, which orders passages by how often they appear in the other questions' gold sets.

**Choices made after inspecting results.** The headline metric was chosen after inspecting the results; the software's configured primary metric was Recall@12. Three configuration choices were made on these same questions after earlier runs (Additional file 1, S1.8):
- the BM25 variant;
- the opening-question weight;
- restricting enrichment to fields the question states.

The ablation is therefore exploratory.

## Outcomes

<!-- 7a, 7c -->

- **Primary:** clinician ratings on each rubric domain, by query area.
- **Secondary:**
  - inter-rater agreement;
  - the accept decision;
  - agreement with the triage category, on assessment items only;
  - ablation MRR@10 and Recall@K for each condition, against both gold standards.

## Statistical analysis

<!-- 7a, 7c, 7d, 7e -->

**Clinician evaluation.** [PLACEHOLDER: to be fixed before rating begins.] We will report:
- the distribution of scores for each domain by area, with medians and interquartile ranges;
- a Bayesian multilevel cumulative-logit model fitted with brms [CITE] to individual (not averaged) ratings.

The model will have fixed effects for domain, area and their interaction, and random intercepts for rater and record. Fitting is described below. From it we will report the posterior probability of a rating of 4 or more for each domain and area, with 95% credible intervals. Contrasts between areas will be exploratory.

Inter-rater agreement will be Krippendorff's α per domain [CITE]: ordinal for the rubric and triage agreement, nominal for the accept decision. The accept decision will be summarised as proportions by area.

**Triage agreement.** For each triage category, we will report the distribution of agreement scores, with the median and interquartile range. Individual ratings will be modelled with a Bayesian cumulative-logit model in brms [CITE], with a fixed effect for triage category and random intercepts for rater and record; as each record had one assessment item, the record intercept is also the item intercept. The scores are treated as ordered categories, without assuming equal spacing. From the model we will report the posterior probability of a rating of 4 or 5 (agree or strongly agree) for each category, with 95% credible intervals. As a sensitivity analysis, the category effect will be allowed to vary across thresholds (category-specific effects), using an adjacent-category logit model, because brms does not allow category-specific effects in cumulative models. Agreement between raters will be Krippendorff's α (ordinal) over the 200 assessment items, overall and by category, reported with the observed percentage agreement, because α can be low when most ratings fall in one category. Comparisons between categories are exploratory, with no significance tests.

**Model fitting.** Both models will use weakly informative priors on the logit scale: Student-t(3, 0, 2.5) for the thresholds, Normal(0, 1.5) for the fixed effects and half-Student-t(3, 0, 2.5) for the random-intercept standard deviations. Each model will be fitted with 4 chains of 4,000 iterations, the first 2,000 of which are warm-up, giving 8,000 posterior draws, with seed 20261027. Convergence will require R-hat ≤1.01, bulk and tail effective sample sizes ≥400 and no divergent transitions; if divergences occur, adapt_delta will be raised from 0.95 to 0.99. Fit will be checked with prior and posterior predictive checks, and prior sensitivity by refitting with Normal(0, 5) priors on the fixed effects. Probabilities of a rating of 4 or more are computed from the posterior draws for a typical rater and record (random effects set to zero) (Additional file 1, S1.11).

**Ablation.** Each comparison varied one factor. A question's score was first averaged over every other free factor, so each question contributed one score to each side. We report means with 95% percentile bootstrap confidence intervals. Differences use a paired bootstrap with a two-sided p-value (10,000 resamples, seed 1234), reported as p<0.001 below the resolution of 0.0001. We also compared the best weight with w = 1; that weight was chosen from the same data, so the comparison is labelled post hoc. No adjustment was made for multiple comparisons, and results by area are exploratory.

## Missing data

<!-- 5e -->

Before the study, 237 of 44,626 admissions (0.53%) with more than 30% of the 14 pSBI-related features missing were excluded from the extract (Additional file 1, S1.1). Within the extract, missing or not-assessed fields were omitted from questions and nothing was imputed; Table 1 reports the missingness. Ablation questions without a gold set were excluded. In the clinician evaluation, items with escalation and "no guideline found" outcomes were kept. [PLACEHOLDER: items with fewer than three ratings.]

## Data and code availability

<!-- 14e, 14f -->

The datasets generated and/or analysed during the current study are not publicly available because of ethical restrictions on sharing the de-identified data set. The dataset comprises patient-level clinical data collected from neonates included in this multicentre study. The primary data are owned by third-party institutions (i.e. the participating hospitals and county governments), with restrictions imposed by the KEMRI Scientific and Ethics Review Unit (SERU) and the Office of Data Protection Kenya (ODPK). The investigators obtained the necessary permissions to access and use these data for the purposes of this research.
